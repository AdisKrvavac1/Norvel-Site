Webflow vs WordPress for B2B marketing sites: how to choose

By Adis KrvavacNovember 17, 20267 min read

Every B2B team rebuilding its website eventually asks the same question: Webflow or WordPress? Both can power a fast, beautiful, search-friendly marketing site. But they make very different trade-offs — and the right choice depends less on feature lists than on who will run the site after launch.

Here is how we think about it after building and redesigning marketing sites for SaaS, AI and enterprise companies.

The short answer

  • Choose Webflow if your marketing team wants to publish and iterate on pages without developers, and your site is primarily a marketing site: pages, landing pages, a blog, case studies.
  • Choose WordPress if you need deep custom functionality, a very large content library with complex workflows, heavy e-commerce, or a specific plugin ecosystem your business depends on.

1. Editing and day-to-day ownership

This is the biggest difference. In Webflow, marketers edit content directly on the page and can build new pages from approved components. Design stays consistent because the building blocks are locked to the design system.

WordPress editing depends heavily on how the site was built. A well-structured block theme can be pleasant to use; a theme stacked with page-builder plugins often isn’t. In practice, many WordPress sites end up needing a developer for changes that Webflow users make themselves.

2. Performance

Webflow serves sites from a global CDN with hosting included, and outputs relatively clean markup. Fast performance is the default; you mainly need to watch image sizes, third-party scripts and heavy animations.

WordPress can be extremely fast, but performance is something you have to engineer: hosting choice, caching, plugin discipline and theme quality all matter. The gap between a well-built and a poorly-built WordPress site is large.

3. Security and maintenance

Webflow is a hosted platform: there are no core, theme or plugin updates to run, and no server to patch. That removes a whole category of maintenance work and risk.

WordPress is self-managed (unless you choose managed hosting). Core, themes and plugins need regular updates, and outdated plugins are a common source of vulnerabilities. It’s manageable — but it is ongoing work someone must own.

4. SEO

Both platforms can rank equally well. Rankings come from content, structure and site quality, not from the CMS logo. Webflow gives you control over metadata, URLs, redirects, sitemaps and custom code for structured data out of the box. WordPress achieves the same through plugins such as Yoast or Rank Math, which add convenience but also another dependency.

5. Flexibility and custom functionality

WordPress wins on raw extensibility. With custom post types, PHP and tens of thousands of plugins, it can become almost anything — membership platforms, complex directories, large stores.

Webflow is deliberately more focused. Custom code, integrations and tools like Memberstack, HubSpot or Zapier cover most marketing needs, but if your website is really a web application, a custom stack or WordPress may be the better foundation.

6. Cost of ownership

Compare total cost, not just licences. Webflow has a predictable subscription that includes hosting. WordPress software is free, but hosting, premium plugins, maintenance and developer time add up. For most marketing sites, the cost that matters most is how often your team has to wait for a developer.

How we decide with clients

We ask four questions:

  1. Who will update the site every week — marketers or developers?
  2. Is this a marketing site, or a product with marketing pages attached?
  3. Which integrations are non-negotiable?
  4. How much content are you migrating, and how is it structured?

For most SaaS, AI and B2B marketing sites we work on, the answers point to Webflow: it lets marketing teams move faster, keeps the design consistent, and removes maintenance overhead. When the answers point elsewhere, we say so.
